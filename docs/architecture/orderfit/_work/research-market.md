### Discrepancy list (ranked by impact on the entry)

1. Submission requirements: PDF says demo video optional and code/security/evidence are listed items; site says video required and code/security self-assessment/execution evidence optional. Submit everything, including a video <=5 min.
2. Bonus: PDF ties 0-5 bonus to on-chain exploration; site English says "advanced topics or demonstrated real-world deployment". Ask organizers whether real deployment on a non-on-chain topic can earn bonus. Do not plan around it.
3. Registration: "passport bio page emailed" not confirmed; site demands ID type/number + enrolment proof via the form emailed to fintechathon@ican-x.com (PDF lists a different contact). Send to both contacts or ask.
4. Deadline wording: 23:59 (PDF) vs 24:00 (site). Treat 23:59 Beijing = 15:59 UTC as the cutoff; the "16:00 UTC" source is the 24:00 wording.
5. ITS repost misstates review (31 Oct) and finalist dates (20 Nov); PDF and site say finalists are announced 31 Oct, check-in 20 Nov. Online review dates differ across sources (22-23 Oct vs 23-28 Oct).
6. Topic E wording differs (site adds "reconciliation"; PDF says financing-option matching + cash-flow with alerts + business financial analysis). Cover the PDF's three pillars; "reconciliation" is optional.
7. Nationality: PDF/site require >=60% non-Chinese with round-up rule; IEEE says "of any nationality" and APEC framing. Follow the 60% rule.
8. Defence date: site says finalists' defence is 22 Nov (closed-door, English); PDF just lists Grand Final 21-23 Nov.

## 3. Manual and workaround workflows

- Spreadsheet plus accountant: weekly (13-week) cash forecast the owner updates by hand, with the new order added as extra rows (deposit in, supplier prepayment out, balance at delivery, net-30 receipt). Claim -> a practical 5-step self-check appears in a search-answer synthesis (not a primary source): start from bank balance and committed payables, add order costs and timing, model customer payment at stated terms and with a 30-day delay, confirm cash never falls below a minimum, and if it does consider deposit, shorter terms or staged delivery -> https://www.kandoo.co.uk/guides/purchase-order-finance-what-is-purchase-order-finance (fetched; covers PO finance, not the 5 steps; the 5 steps came from a WebSearch summary, so low-quality) -> low.
- PO financing as the default fix for goods-based orders: advance up to 90% of order value to 100% of supplier costs, no standard figure, each deal underwritten; fees "commonly quoted from around 2% to 5%" of financed amount; needs a confirmed PO and creditworthy end buyer; not suitable for services; funds supplier cost only; late customer payment builds fees -> https://www.kandoo.co.uk/guides/purchase-order-finance-what-is-purchase-order-finance (a UK lender-side guide, MARKETING-adjacent) -> med.
- A HubSpot piece gives a different fee range (1-6% per 30 days, i.e. roughly 20-60% APR) -> https://blog.hubspot.com/sales/purchase-order-financing (SEARCH-ONLY, not fetched) -> low. The two sources disagree; do not hard-code a PO financing price in OrderFit. Use a user-supplied financing quote, which matches the brief ("user-supplied financing").
- BDC explainer: URL I tried (bdc.ca purchase-order-financing-explained) returned 404, so not verified.
- Build-your-own LLM scripts: several vendor blueprints exist for "AI cash flow agent" (e.g. https://resources.rework.com/libraries/ai-agents/ai-cash-flow-agent, SEARCH-ONLY, vendor marketing). They forecast and flag, not execute; they need live bank/AR/AP data -> low.

## 4. SME practice evidence

Claim -> evidence -> confidence. Many sources are vendors or journalism; labels given.

- Half of small businesses hold under about 4 weeks of cash buffer: JPMorgan Chase Institute 2016 "Cash is King" found median 27 buffer days (597,000 small businesses, Feb-Oct 2015); employer small businesses median 18 days; a later metro-areas piece says 50% have fewer than 15 buffer days from a different data set. Search-result summary of the Institute pages, not fetched; data is 2015-era -> https://www.jpmorganchase.com/corporate/institute/report-cash-flows-balances-and-buffer-days.htm ; https://www.jpmorganchase.com/institute/all-topics/business-growth-and-entrepreneurship/small-business-cash-liquidity-in-25-metro-areas -> med (primary institution, but not fetched; numbers conflict, state as "roughly 2-4 weeks, dated").
- Half of suppliers are paid late: Taulia (SAP) Supplier Survey 2023, 9,600+ responses; respondents are users of an early-payment platform, so biased sample and vendor-sponsored -> https://taulia.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/1548810_SupplierSurvey2023_021723.pdf (SEARCH-ONLY summary) -> low-med.
- Late payment prevalence in Australia: a Feb 2025 Moneytech/CreditorWatch analysis says late payments reached the highest rate since 2021; construction, manufacturing, transport, wholesale among worst -> https://www.accountantsdaily.com.au/business/20985-cash-flow-late-payment-struggles-set-to-persist-for-smes-says-moneytech (SEARCH-ONLY, trade press/finance provider) -> low.
- UK: CICM says certainty of payment date matters more to SMEs than the headline terms -> channelweb summary (SEARCH-ONLY) -> low.
- No hard statistic found for how often SMEs take deposits on large orders or how they decide to accept an order. -> UNVERIFIED. (Search explicitly returned none.)
- Widely repeated "X% of small businesses fail from cash flow" statistics were not retrieved or verified; do not use.
- Where it breaks (qualitative, supported by the PO-finance source above): financing covers supplier cost only, so freight/duty/overheads need other cash; margin must absorb fee; customer late payment extends financing period; PO finance excludes services. -> kandoo guide -> med.
- Bangladesh/emerging-market context (short, low-med confidence): RMG exporters rely on back-to-back LCs, settled when export proceeds are realised; in 2025 reports, exporters complained of banks delaying disbursement of repatriated proceeds, hindering LC settlement; Bangladesh Textile Mills Association reported about $270 million of bills unpaid across banks (search-summary of fibre2fashion/Daily Star/apparelresources) -> https://www.fibre2fashion.com/news/apparel-news/banking-woes-threaten-bangladesh-s-rmg-export-momentum-305258-newsdetails.htm (SEARCH-ONLY, trade press) -> low-med. An industry warning says non-bonded apparel factories source "70-80%" of raw materials on credit via back-to-back LC (undated; SEARCH-ONLY, tbsnews.net/node/299899) -> low. Advance-payment norms in BD: not found -> UNVERIFIED. SME financing gap: World Bank estimate $2.8B cited by TBS News (SEARCH-ONLY) -> https://www.tbsnews.net/supplement/difficult-period-bangladeshs-small-businesses-885616 -> low-med.
- Practical implication for design: user inputs (deposit %, supplier terms, customer payment delay) are uncertain and vary; the timeline should show a delay sensitivity (for example +30 days late payment) rather than one number. The late-payment sources support this need qualitatively.

## 6. Failure-mode references

- OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications: current version is the 2025 list (site shows "LLM Top 10 for 2025" with link to the earlier 2023-24 list) -> https://genai.owasp.org/llm-top-10/ (fetched; only teaser text shown) -> high on version, med on item detail. IDs: LLM01 Prompt Injection, LLM02 Sensitive Information Disclosure, LLM03 Supply Chain, LLM04 Data and Model Poisoning, LLM05 Improper Output Handling, LLM06 Excessive Agency, LLM07 System Prompt Leakage, LLM08 Vector and Embedding Weaknesses, LLM09 Misinformation, LLM10 Unbounded Consumption.
- Items relevant to a document-reading finance agent:
  - LLM01 Prompt Injection: instructions hidden in an order PO/contract/invoice PDF or email can steer the agent (indirect injection). Design consequence: treat uploaded documents as data; extract into a typed schema; never let document text choose tools or terms.
  - LLM05 Improper Output Handling: model output passed to downstream components without validation. Consequence: validate extracted numbers/dates against schema and re-compute totals in deterministic code.
  - LLM06 Excessive Agency: too much functionality/permission/autonomy. Consequence: OrderFit should be read-only/advisory, with no payment or bank actions; if any action exists, tier it.
  - LLM09 Misinformation (and LLM02 Sensitive Information Disclosure): fabricated figures presented confidently; leakage of financial data. Consequence: compute cash timeline in code, not in the LLM; cite source document fields for every number; keep per-user data isolation.
- NIST AI 600-1, Generative AI Profile of the AI RMF, July 2024 (READ the PDF text locally via pdftotext): 12 risks, section numbers 2.1-2.12 (CBRN; Confabulation; Dangerous/Violent/Hateful Content; Data Privacy; Environmental; Harmful Bias and Homogenization; Human-AI Configuration; Information Integrity; Information Security; Intellectual Property; Obscene/Degrading/Abusive; Value Chain and Component Integration). Relevant: 2.2 Confabulation ("generate and confidently present erroneous or false content"), 2.7 Human-AI Configuration (automation bias, over-reliance), 2.9 Information Security (names direct and indirect prompt injection; indirect = adversary-controlled data retrieved by the system), 2.4 Data Privacy -> https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf and https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1 -> high.

## 7. Honest verdict

Is OrderFit's specific capability differentiated?

- Timeline-only (enter an order's cash events, see a dated cash line with a minimum-cash day): NOT differentiated. At least Dryrun, Float, Pulse and Cash Flow Frog advertise manual what-if scenarios with dated cash views, and Dryrun explicitly mentions adjusting "payment timing" and adding project inflows/outflows before invoices exist (marketing, but plausible). A spreadsheet plus accountant does the same.
- "Accept or decline" decision on a single new order against existing obligations: THIN. I found no verified product that frames the decision as accept/decline for an order, but it is a framing of the same what-if capability; the user can already do it by hand.
- Automatic generation and ranking of term changes (customer deposit/milestones, supplier terms, staged delivery, user-supplied financing, defer/decline) that restore feasibility while preserving profit, with a delta timeline per option: not confirmed in any product I fetched. Xero JAX "Cash Flow Actions" is the nearest pattern ("proposes the fix") but its levers are bills and payroll, and its scope for orders is not stated. -> This is where OrderFit could be differentiated, med-low confidence because absence of evidence from ~20 pages and searches is not proof.
- Also potentially additive: profitability and obligations check in one pass (order margin after financing cost, against existing payables, payroll and loan repayments); explicit assumptions and delay sensitivity; auditable per-number provenance from the source documents; an English-first, emerging-market-friendly input (manual/CSV/document upload) not tied to Xero/QuickBooks. The Bangladesh product I could verify (Bisnesy) is POS/ledger, with no forecasting, so BD SMEs likely lack ledger-integrated forecasting; low confidence.

What it could add concretely: a term-change solver with a dated before/after timeline and "what must be true" conditions per option; honest refusal when infeasible; a human-confirmed output (no money movement); deterministic arithmetic with LLM only used to read documents and explain.

Competition fit: the rubric rewards safe, verifiable task completion and clarification/fallback over novelty of finance maths, so thin differentiation vs commercial tools matters less than clean sandboxed end-to-end scripts, injection-resistant document reading, and visible permission/confirmation design.

Smallest pivot if judged "weak": reposition from "affordability check" to "order-term negotiation planner": input = one new order + current obligations; output = 3 ranked counter-offers (e.g. 30% deposit, split delivery, supplier net-30 request) each with the dated cash timeline and the minimum balance date, plus a plain-language script the owner can send. That keeps the unproven-in-market part (term options) as the headline and treats the timeline as a supporting chart.
