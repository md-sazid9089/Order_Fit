## Net effect on the design

The architecture did not change (Option B stands), but three claims did. The robustness claim of the anchor is weaker and now honest: the recommended plan holds a 15-day customer delay with the other customers 5 days late, with 71,200.00 of headroom, and **nothing survives the severe case**; the agent says so. The security claim is narrower and testable: a structural property (the model has no tools, only owner-confirmed typed fields reach the engine, the approval binds the exact actions) plus evidence that can fail (live adversarial runs, value-manipulation documents, a red-team script). The scope is smaller (three levers cut, five conveniences moved to IF-TIME) and the engine is reused rather than re-implemented.

The reviewer's point that a five-line greedy rule often matches the engine is reported in [05](../05-security-and-evaluation.md) section 6.4 with the measured match rate, and the demo is built around the cases where it fails.

## Items the reviewer could not verify (carried forward)

All `[W]` claims (competitors, rules, prices, versions) were not re-fetched by the reviewer; the repository code was out of its scope; real extraction quality and PDF grounding brittleness are inferences until the synthetic PDFs are run.
