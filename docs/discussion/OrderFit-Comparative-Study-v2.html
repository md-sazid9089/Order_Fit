Chapter 1 · Start here

A sale is not the same as money in the bank

A business can receive a large order today, spend money preparing it next week, and get paid much later. The order may earn money overall while leaving too little cash for bills along the way.

Profit

Will the order earn more than it costs?

Profit is about what remains after costs. It does not tell you whether the business has enough money on the day a bill is due.

Cash flow

When will money arrive and leave?

follows payment dates. A future customer payment cannot pay today's supplier bill.

What OrderFit is trying to achieve

OrderFit would help small businesses decide whether they can afford to fulfil a new customer order and which payment terms or financing would make it manageable.

It would consider the order alongside available money and existing commitments. The owner would compare options, understand their risks, and decide whether to accept, negotiate or decline.

Status: this describes the intended project, not a working product or a proven business opportunity.

Chapter 3 · The existing landscape

What tools already help with this?

Different tools solve different parts of the problem. Some help you see future cash. Some run financial tasks. Others provide money. Click a question to understand the distinction.

Three kinds of help, one business decision

Forecasting tools show the timing; finance platforms connect analysis with work; financing providers change when money is available. OrderFit would try to bring the relevant alternatives into one order decision.

Chapter 5 · Where we might add value

Help the owner reach workable terms, not just read a chart

The research suggests a narrower opportunity: connect one incoming customer order to the financial changes needed to fulfil it. These opportunities still need user validation.

Proposed opportunity

Identify the terms needed to proceed

Help the owner understand the advance, supplier payment date or funding amount required to keep existing commitments manageable.

Why it matters — and the catch

It converts a money gap into a commercial question. But a customer or supplier may reject those terms. “It works if agreed” is different from “it has been agreed.”

Proposed opportunity

Compare borrowing with negotiation

Put customer advances, supplier extensions, delivery changes and available financing on the same basis.

Why it matters — and the catch

Borrowing should not be the default solution. Negotiation also has costs: discounts, relationship pressure and lost sales. The comparison must account for them.

Proposed opportunity

Explain how much delay the plan can withstand

Show when a change in customer timing or costs would make the selected plan stop working.

Why it matters — and the catch

This makes the recommendation conditional and understandable. A stress scenario is not a prediction of its likelihood, and cannot establish that an order is safe under every circumstance.

Proposed opportunity

Reassess when someone changes the terms

Track proposed terms, counteroffers and accepted agreements, then update the financial assessment.

Why it matters — and the catch

A prepared message is only one step. The decision changes when the reply changes. An old approval must not authorise a revised plan.

Proposed opportunity

Separate a possible plan from a ready plan

Make missing records, unconfirmed advances and unapproved financing visible before the owner commits.

Why it matters — and the catch

The numbers can work while the evidence does not. The system must be willing to ask for more information or decline to recommend.

Proposed opportunity

Leave a clear explanation of the decision

Record the selected plan, rejected options, assumptions and changes that would require another review.

Why it matters — and the catch

Useful for an owner and accountant to inspect later. Source-linked explanations already exist elsewhere; the value must come from the complete order context.

The distinction we should test

OrderFit would help a business determine the financial terms under which a particular customer order becomes affordable, then prepare the steps needed to secure those terms.

We did not find reviewed documentation clearly packaging that entire sequence for small-business owners. This is a possible gap in the workflow, not proof that no competitor does it or that customers will pay for it.

What could make the idea fail?
  • Incomplete records: missing wages, taxes or debt payments can invalidate a correct-looking order calculation.
  • Already good enough: the owner's current accountant, spreadsheet or AI assistant may handle the decision adequately.
  • Too broad: manufacturing, wholesale and service businesses have different costs and terms. Start with one accessible segment.
  • Unavailable funding: a catalogue offer is not approval; a customer invoice may not exist when upfront costs are due.
  • Persuasive but untested: a fictional demonstration cannot prove usefulness with messy real documents.

How we could test the idea fairly

Give the same records to OrderFit, an experienced person using a spreadsheet, and an existing AI assistant. Independently check the outcomes. Compare correctness, time taken, missing-information detection and how well the owner understands the result.

Use unfamiliar documents and real workflow feedback. A fictional test can check arithmetic; it cannot establish customer demand or reliable extraction across real businesses.

Reference desk

Financial terms in everyday language

Open a term below. Underlined terms elsewhere in the guide open the same explanation.

Cash flow

The movement of money into and out of a business, with the dates included. It answers when money is actually available, rather than just whether a sale is profitable.

Profit

What remains after the relevant costs are deducted from income. A positive profit does not mean that money is available before the customer pays.

Cash reserve

Money the owner wants to keep available for existing commitments and unexpected needs. The right amount depends on the business; the guide’s $2,000 is invented.

Customer advance

Part of a customer’s agreed payment received before delivery or completion. It brings money forward; it does not increase the total sale price.

Financing

A way to obtain money to run or grow the business. Terms, costs, timing and eligibility differ. Borrowing creates a repayment obligation; other arrangements can work differently.

Working capital

Resources available to support everyday operations. In this guide, working-capital financing refers to money used to bridge the gap between paying costs and collecting customer payments.

Accounts receivable

Money customers owe the business. A customer invoice is a record of that amount; it is not the same as money already received.

Accounts payable

Money the business owes suppliers or other vendors. The amount and due date matter when planning cash.

Purchase order

A document stating goods or services being ordered and their terms. An incoming customer order and an order the business issues to its supplier are different sides of a trade.

Invoice

A request for payment for supplied goods or services. Some costs must be paid before an invoice can be raised to the customer.

Cash-flow forecast

An estimate of future money received and paid. Its usefulness depends on the records and assumptions underneath it. It cannot guarantee what will happen.

Scenario

An alternative set of assumptions used to explore what would change. Comparing scenarios shows consequences, not how likely each future is.

Baseline

The starting picture of the business without the proposed change. Missing commitments in the baseline can make every alternative look better than it really is.

Payment terms / Net 30

Rules about when payment is due. Net 30 means payment is due 30 days after a specified starting event. The document must establish that event; do not guess it.

Liquidity

The ability to obtain or use money when a payment is due. A valuable asset or profitable order may not provide cash at the time it is needed.

Deterministic calculation

A calculation that follows explicit rules and gives the same result for the same inputs. Correct arithmetic still produces a misleading answer if the inputs or rules are wrong.

AI connector / MCP

A way for an AI assistant to use information and tools from another application. It does not by itself prove the financial result is correct or that every action is safe.

Sandbox

A simulated or isolated environment for demonstrations and testing. Recording a fictional payment in a sandbox does not move real money or secure real financing.

What this research can and cannot tell us

What was checked

Official product pages, help articles, developer documentation, release notes and company announcements. Each product is currently marketed with accessible documentation.

The study covers nine principal products and two adjacent platforms. It is a selected comparison, not a complete list of every competitor.

What remains unknown

Paid accounts were not tested. Product claims are not independent performance results. We did not prove that the whole OrderFit workflow is absent from the market or that owners want a separate product.

Availability varies by region and plan. The product research dates to 13 October 2026; future releases may change the comparison.
